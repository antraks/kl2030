Food & drink

In Kuala Lumpur, people eat at any hour, outdoors and for a few ringgit. Malay, Chinese and Indian cooking meet on every street corner. Here is how to make the most of it during the Games, at the venues and in town.

The essentials

What to know before you enter a venue.

RM 12

The Rimau plate

Every official stall offers a dish and a drink for 12 ringgit, at the same price at every venue.

Water is free

Fountains at every entrance and in every stand. Bring an empty bottle: afternoons are well above 30 °C.

Bringing your own food

Allowed, in a closed container and for your own consumption. Glass and alcohol are not allowed.

Cashless venues

Stalls take bank cards and mobile payment. Stalls in the food villages also accept cash.

Everything is halal

All official stalls are halal-certified. No alcohol is sold at the venues.

Reusable tableware

Plates, bowls and cups are reusable, with an RM 2 deposit. Return them to any stall.

Three food villages

One large food area per hub, open every day of the Games.

Pasar Rimau

Bukit Jalil · 10 am – midnight · free entry

The Games' night market, on the concourse between the National Stadium and Axiata Arena. Forty stalls, shaded tables and giant screens to follow the finals.

Commonwealth Street

City centre · 11 am – 11 pm · free entry

In KLCC Park, six nations in the spotlight every day: the 74 cuisines of the Commonwealth in twelve days, from New Zealand meat pies to Jamaican jerk chicken.

Medan Selera MITEC

Greater KL · with a MITEC ticket

The food court shared by weightlifting, wrestling and judo. The more distant venues each have their own stall, and the shuttle departure stations have kiosks.

Official food stalls

At least one stall per venue. They open with the gates and close 30 minutes after the last event.

    Dishes to try

    Ten classics you will find at the venues and on the street.

    • Nasi lemak

      Rice cooked in coconut milk and pandan, with sambal, fried anchovies, peanuts and egg. The national dish, eaten at breakfast as well as at midnight.

      • Spicy
      • Peanuts

    • Roti canai

      Flaky flatbread cooked on a griddle and dipped in dhal or curry. Roti telur is the version with egg.

      • Vegetarian

    • Satay

      Skewers marinated in turmeric, grilled over charcoal and served with peanut sauce and cubes of pressed rice.

      • Peanuts

    • Char kway teow

      Flat rice noodles stir-fried in a very hot wok with prawns, cockles, bean sprouts and chives.

    • Laksa

      Noodle soup. In Kuala Lumpur it comes with curry and coconut milk; in Penang it is sour, made with fish and tamarind.

      • Spicy

    • Rendang

      Beef simmered for hours in coconut milk and spices, until the sauce is almost dry.

      • Spicy

    • Nasi kandar

      Rice topped with several curries mixed together, the speciality of mamak restaurants, run by Indian Muslim families.

      • Spicy

    • Teh tarik

      “Pulled” milk tea: poured from a height, from one jug to another, until it froths.

      • Vegetarian

    • Cendol

      Shaved ice, coconut milk, palm sugar and little green pandan jelly noodles. The best cure for the heat.

      • Vegan

    • Kuih

      Colourful little cakes made with glutinous rice, coconut and pandan, sold by the piece. Take several to share.

      • Vegetarian

    Diets and allergies

    Vegetarian: easy, especially in South Indian cooking. Banana leaf rice and roti canai with dhal contain no meat.

    Gluten-free: harder. Soy sauce and wheat noodles are everywhere; go for rice and rice noodles.

    Peanuts: they are everywhere, in nasi lemak, satay and rojak. On this page, stalls that serve them are marked. If you have a severe allergy, say so clearly at the stall and carry your medication.

    Eating in town

    After the evening sessions, four neighbourhoods where the stalls stay open late.

    • Jalan Alor

      Bukit Bintang

      The street of grills and seafood, which comes alive at nightfall. Noisy, smoky, unmissable.

      Monorail or MRT, Bukit Bintang station

    • Petaling Street

      Chinatown

      Under the market's great glass roof, stalls selling noodles, roast duck and roasted chestnuts.

      LRT or MRT, Pasar Seni station

    • Brickfields

      Little India

      Banana leaf rice, dosas and Indian sweets, a stone's throw from KL Sentral. A paradise for vegetarians.

      KL Sentral, then a 5-minute walk

    • Kampung Baru

      Malay village

      Wooden houses at the foot of the Petronas Towers. Nasi lemak, satay and home cooking.

      LRT Kelana Jaya Line, Kampung Baru station
